# Born in 1975. Lived Through Five Computing Revolutions. The Sixth is Here.

**Published: May 8, 2026**

I grew up when computers filled rooms. Today, I carry more power in my pocket than NASA used to land on the moon.

Jensen Huang talks about the five layers of AI infrastructure. But zoom out, and you see something bigger: **we're stacking revolutions.**

- **1980s**: Personal computers → put the machine on your desk
- **1990s**: Internet → connected those machines
- **2000s**: Cloud → made computing infinite and invisible  
- **2010s**: Mobile → put it in your pocket, always on
- **2020s**: AI → the machine now understands *intent*, not just commands

Each layer didn't replace the last. **It compounded it.**

What strikes me most: the time from *problem to solution* has collapsed. 

In the 80s, you learned to code to make the computer do something. In the 90s, you searched the web for answers. In the 2000s, you found software in the cloud. In the 2010s, there was an app for that.

Now? You describe what you need, and AI helps you build it.

---

## Here's what this means for design:

We've gone from "design the interface" to **"design the intelligence."** 

The tools we build today help us build better tools tomorrow. Figma was designed in design tools. AI models are trained by AI. Every solution becomes the foundation for the next problem we couldn't even see before.

As a designer who's adapted through every shift, I've never felt more energized. Not because the tools are better—but because **the problems we can now tackle are bigger.**

AI doesn't replace design thinking. It removes the friction between *having an idea* and *testing if it works*. That compression of time unlocks entirely new categories of problems to solve.

We're not just making things faster. We're making *new things possible.*

---

## The pattern I see: 

Each computing revolution made creation more accessible. 

- Personal computers: you didn't need a university lab.  
- Internet: you didn't need a publisher.  
- Cloud: you didn't need a server room.  
- Mobile: you didn't need to be at your desk.  
- AI: you don't need to be an expert in everything.

**Design has never been more accessible to practitioners.** And that means the diversity of problems we solve explodes.

The next breakthrough won't come from someone who lived through all five eras like me. It'll come from someone who was *born into the AI era* and sees possibilities the rest of us can't imagine yet.

That's what excites me most.

Every morning, I open tools built by tools, to solve problems that didn't exist when I woke up yesterday.
